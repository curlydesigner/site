<a id="uxresearch"></a>

#### UX Research

<br>

**How do you approach user research and ensure it informs your designs?**

Understanding the project’s goals and constraints. Depending on the context, I use a mix of methods like interviews, surveys, usability testing, and analyzing analytics. For example, when designing a feature for a workflow management tool, I conducted interviews with end-users to uncover pain points and tested prototypes to validate assumptions. The insights gained helped me prioritize design decisions and resulted in a highly intuitive solution that aligned with user expectations.

<br>

**How do you measure the success of your designs?**

I use both qualitative and quantitative metrics to track success. 
For Qualitative feedback, I rely on usability testing and interviews for user satisfaction. 
On the Quantitative side, I monitor KPIs like task completion rates, error rates, and engagement metrics post-launch. 

 It’s like solving puzzles and pulling numerous details into a clear strategy. It's the ability to seamlessly blend functionality with delight and create an experience that users find easy to navigate and enjoyable, without overwhelming or confusing them. But it is also the result of a team collaboration.

<a id="accessibility"></a>

### Accessibility 

<br>

Accessibility has a significant impact on the user experience of players. Readability, text size, icons, touch target for actions, and spacing can affect the accessibility and usability of an interface. The challenge of displaying a large amount of information that needs to appear on a small mobile map, gameplay, means Navigation needs to be simplified and clear from their layout and assessability. Therefore, I took the time to create guidelines and templates that can be followed to ensure optimal touch target usability. Text, images, icons, and other elements are clear and straightforward with considerable leveraging and ‘white space.’
