Outsmart / 3GSM

The Design Challenge

Bring brand, products, and people into one experience.

The exhibition needed to communicate the company's identity, present products clearly, support digital exploration, and create space for both quick visitor interactions and longer sales or service conversations.

The work combined the concerns of spatial design and UX: visibility, wayfinding, interaction points, information hierarchy, and the transition from self-directed exploration to human conversation.

From Logo to Spatial Concept

The exhibition form grew from the shape and geometry of the company logo.

The Outsmart exhibition concept connected brand identity, spatial form, product information, interactive screens, and conversation areas into one visitor experience for 3GSM World Congress in Barcelona, Spain.

Logo Geometry

Study the visual form of the mark: curves, repeated shapes, angles, proportions, and the red-and-white brand rhythm.

→

Exploratory Sketches

→

Spatial Form

Translate the selected geometry into a booth footprint, structural volume, product wall, and service counter.

Visitor Experience

Plan how people enter, scan, explore product information, use screens, sit, discuss, and continue the relationship.

Footprint and Circulation

The form organized movement from the open aisle into product exploration, touchscreens, and seated conversations.

Information Hierarchy

Large brand surfaces worked from a distance, while product graphics, displays, and screen content supported closer inspection.

Physical + Digital System

Kiosks, touchscreens, product walls, and service seating were planned as connected parts of the same visitor journey.

Designing the Visitor Journey

Physical and digital touchpoints worked together instead of acting as separate moments.

Visitors could read the brand from the aisle, enter the space through a clear visual path, explore products through displays and screens, then move into sales or service conversations when they needed more context.

Digital Layer

Touchscreens, kiosks, and interactive product information extended the physical booth into a self-guided information layer.

Explore

Product displays supported quick scanning and self-directed discovery before a visitor needed staff support.

Interact

Touchscreens and kiosks let visitors access additional product information at the point of interest.

Discuss

Sales seating and counters created natural places for deeper conversations after initial exploration.

Connect

Service areas allowed visitors to ask questions, continue the relationship, and move from interest to next steps.

Remember

The strong overhead form and repeated brand geometry made the booth recognizable within a dense exhibition hall.

Design Decisions and Outcome

The final exhibition was constructed and used in a live exhibition environment at the 3GSM World Congress in Barcelona, Spain.

Brand as Structure

The company mark informed the booth's visible shape, not just the graphics applied to it.

Digital as Context

Screens and kiosks supported product exploration at the moment visitors were already looking, touching, and asking questions.

Service as Space

Conversation areas were planned as part of the experience, giving sales and support interactions a clear place in the journey.

Kramer Electronics

Kramer Electronics Exhibition

A modular exhibition system for product discovery, digital displays, and business conversations.

Kramer Electronics had a large product portfolio and needed a portable exhibition environment that could organize multiple departments while staying open, visible, and useful for business visitors.

Designed within Barzilai Design Studio, the concept included Kramer Tools, Kramer Home, new collections, a meeting area, a welcome stand, a central bar, screens on multiple sides, hidden storage, and a dedicated area for the SierraVideo sub-brand.

The Design Challenge

Create a movable booth that felt structured, interactive, and welcoming.

The challenge was to design a booth that could be disassembled and transported while still feeling like a cohesive branded environment. It needed to attract visitors, support product demos, hide storage, and create comfortable places for business conversations.

Developing the Spatial System

The form, zones, and visitor path were developed as one experience system.

The concept balanced a clear central gathering point with perimeter product zones, visible digital displays, and branded thresholds that helped visitors understand where to go next.

Initial Inspiration

Start from Kramer brand requirements, product families, and the need for a portable exhibition structure.

→

Early Sketches

→

Functional Zoning

Separate product families while keeping sightlines open from the central meeting and bar area.

→

Visitor Circulation

Invite people in from multiple sides, let them discover products independently, then guide them toward demos and discussions.
